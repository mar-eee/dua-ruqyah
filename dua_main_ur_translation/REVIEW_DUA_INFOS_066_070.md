# Urdu review — dua_infos 066–070

Scope: five chunks, eight item-parts and 13 target fields, covering records 38–42 and finishing the `dua_infos` chunk series. Each field was compared with its Bengali source and read independently for contemporary Pakistani Urdu. Source, frozen fields, IDs, split order and tag sequences remain unchanged. Five protected Arabic blocks match the source character for character. This is an editorial/automated review, not independent human scholarly sign-off.

## Source and meaning decisions

- **066 / record 38 — hope and distress.** The Bengali source describes depression as usually spiritual and equates being depressed with disbelief. Urdu retains the author's emphasis on remembrance and hope, but attributes the causal claims to him and avoids applying a Quranic warning to a person with a clinical illness. [Quran 12:87](https://quran.com/12/87), [2:268](https://quran.com/2/268) and [94:5–6](https://quran.com/94/5-6) were checked for meaning; the two supplied Arabic blocks were not repaired or normalized. The cited [Abu Dawud 4993](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4993) says that good opinion is part of good worship and is placed under good opinion of people; it does **not** verify the Bengali's Allah-specific wording. The Urdu quotes only the general wording and explains the distinction. The separate servant-expectation report is paraphrased, not merged into Abu Dawud 4993.
- **067 / record 39 — gratitude.** The excerpt of [Quran 14:7](https://quran.com/14/7) and its meaning were checked. The report on thanking people agrees with [Tirmidhi 1954](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1954). The kindness-and-recompense report is [Abu Dawud 1672](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1672), not the Bengali's [2131](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2131), which concerns marriage. The source's Nasa'i 587 points to a prayer-time report and remains flagged rather than used to authenticate this narration. The two Ahmad gratitude reports and their source-attributed grading still need edition-level quotation review; no new grading is asserted.
- **068 / record 40 — zuhd and conduct.** The mat report matches [Tirmidhi 2377](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2377); the traveler saying and Ibn Umar's subsequent advice were distinguished using [Bukhari 6416](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6416), correcting the source's 1283. The source token 21373 beside Ibn Majah is corrupt bibliographic OCR, so it is not presented as a second hadith number. [Ibn Majah 4102](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:4102) and [Tirmidhi 2465](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2465) have grading differences from the Bengali's blanket *sahih* labels; the Urdu attributes those labels to the author. [Tirmidhi 2003](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2003) is graded *hasan* in the checked edition, while [2018](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2018) supports the good-character contrast. The claim about an affable believer and its exact secondary citation remain for edition-level review.
- **069 / records 41–42 — fasting, charity, night prayer.** The many fasting schedules and narratives of household austerity are translated as source descriptions, not declared equally established fixed Sunnah rules. Bengali `112` o'clock is obvious OCR corruption and is rendered `1 or 2` o'clock. The author's strict post-sleep use of “tahajjud” is attributed as terminology rather than imposed as an uncontested ruling. The Amr ibn Abasah wording was compared with [Tirmidhi 3579](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3579): it is **the Lord** who is nearest His servant in the last part of the night. The best-night-prayer report is [Muslim 1163](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1163b), not the source's 2821.
- **070 / record 42 — night prayer continued.** The Abdullah ibn Salam report in [Tirmidhi 2485](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2485) has peace, feeding people and prayer at night, but lacks the source's kinship clause; an [alternate cited version](https://sunnah.com/urn/2118490) includes it. Urdu preserves the clause while identifying the variant. Aisha's advice not to abandon night prayer agrees with [Abu Dawud 1307](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1307). The long-recitation and four practical steps are all retained. The Abu Umamah nighttime-prayer wording, disease-removal addition and its claimed grading need exact edition-level review; the translated text does not present the addition as universally verified.
