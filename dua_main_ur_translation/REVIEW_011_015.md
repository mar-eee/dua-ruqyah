## Fresh corrections and conflict decisions

- Chunks 011–012 required no target changes after the fresh source comparison;
  their 23 and 40 translated fields, respectively, were nevertheless reviewed
  in full rather than carried forward from the earlier claim.
- In row 163, repaired the incomplete Urdu perfect construction in the
  instruction following the mu'adhdhin's testimony.
- In row 168, restored `رسول اللہ ﷺ نے فرمایا` as the speaker of the report.
  The English field omits that link, while the Arabic chain and matn of
  [Abu Dawud 517](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:517) explicitly contain it.
- In row 172, made `یہ دعا` the item written on a sheet and sealed; the former
  pronoun grammatically made the person himself the object being written.
- In row 177, corrected the narration's tense and subject so that the Prophet ﷺ
  reads the following words, rather than abruptly instructing the reader.
- In row 179, removed the unsupported added `سلام` from the invocation
  `اللهم صل على محمد`, while retaining the request for Allah's bounty. The
  latter is confirmed by [Muslim 713a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:713a).
- In row 189, replaced the stiff and temporally incomplete wording for earlier
  and later sins with natural Urdu that preserves both clauses.
- In nested row 192, changed the user-facing instruction from singular casual
  address to the batch's respectful instructional register. Its translation
  still correctly renders `نفثه` as the devil's spitting, not his “voice.”
- Retitled row 194 as an exhortation to complete ruku and sujud, because its
  two reports contain no dua. The already corrected narrators Zaid ibn Wahb
  رحمہ اللہ and Abu Qatadah رضی اللہ عنہ were rechecked against
  [Bukhari 791](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:791) and the cited Mishkat report.
- In row 202, replaced the inverted calque with the natural established sense:
  `اللہ نے اُس شخص کی سن لی جس نے اس کی حمد کی۔`

Field-specific decisions for rows 168, 179, and 194 are recorded in
`GLOSSARY.json`. The prior row 144, 189, 192, 194, and 199 conflict decisions
were rechecked and remain in force.
