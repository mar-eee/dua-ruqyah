# Urdu review — dua_infos 051–055

Scope: five chunks, 16 item-parts and 17 translated fields (16 descriptions and one name). Record 33 continues from 050 and ends in 055; record 34 begins in 055 and continues in 056. All requested targets are filled. Source text, keys, IDs, frozen fields, chunk boundaries and HTML tag order remain unchanged. Both protected Arabic passages are copied character for character.

## Meaning and source decisions

- In 051, the discussion of praying for absent Muslims and of Allah's names is translated in full. The meaning of [Quran 7:180](https://quran.com/7/180) was checked against the verse; the Urdu is a rendering of meaning, not a quotation from an identified published Urdu translation. The Bengali citation for the 99-names report gives Bukhari **2585**, which is [a report about gifts](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2585) in modern numbering. The Urdu uses the matching [Bukhari 2736](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2736). That single numeric correction is recorded in `GLOSSARY.json`; other numeric values were compared part by part.
- In 052, the source's printed Tirmidhi reference for *Ya Dhal-Jalali wal-Ikram* appears OCR-damaged and is retained rather than guessed. [Tirmidhi 3525](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3525) has the same saying through Anas, not the source's Rabi'ah bin Amir, and Tirmidhi himself describes that route as *gharib* and not preserved despite the source's unqualified sahih label. The Urdu explicitly attributes the sahih claim to the author. The use of particular closing formulas is conveyed as the author's guidance, not an uncontested rule.
- In 053–054, the qiblah and hand-raising recommendations remain the author's contextual rulings. The source itself reports disagreement about wiping the face after dua; the Urdu preserves each named scholar's assessment without silently resolving the debate. The bare `225` after the Abdul Razzaq report in the source is retained as a bracketed source marker, not given an invented bibliographic identity. The rain-prayer account was checked against [Bukhari 1029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1029), whose chapter and wording match the source quotation.
- In 055, the source introduces the shoulder-height, single-finger and extended-hands report as a saying of the Prophet. [Abu Dawud 1489](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1489) records it as Ibn Abbas's own statement, so Urdu corrects the attribution without changing the citation number. The warning about looking upward in prayer matches [Muslim 429](https://sunnah.com/muslim:429). The opening of record 34 presents the author's theological distinction between ordinary and supernatural assistance as his view, without declaring specific people unbelievers. Its excerpt from [Quran 5:75–76](https://quran.com/5/75-76) is rendered as meaning and preserves the excerpt boundary.
