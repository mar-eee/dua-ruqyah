## Meaning and source decisions

- In 056 the author's distinction between asking visible people for ordinary help, asking unseen beings, and invoking a particular absent person is fully represented. These are contested theological claims and are attributed to the author, not presented as a judgment on any identifiable person's faith. The wilderness-help narration is not certified authentic: the source itself cites al-Albani's *Silsilat al-Daifah* 2/108–110, no. 655. The permissibility claim needs qualified scholarly review before release.
- In 057 the Bengali quotation of Thawban's pledge loses the negation and consequently reverses the sense. The Urdu restores “will **not** ask people,” matching [Abu Dawud 1643](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1643) and the surrounding narrative. The source field is untouched. The further counsel to confide one's pain chiefly to Allah remains the author's exhortation, not a prohibition on seeking legitimate human help.
- In 058 the Bengali attributes “the best of you is one who learns the Quran and teaches it” to Abu Hurayrah; [Bukhari 5027](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5027) names Uthman ibn Affan. The Urdu corrects the narrator without altering the source. The verse-learning and she-camel comparisons were checked against [Muslim 803](https://sunnah.com/muslim:803). The anecdote about the title *hafiz* remains recognizably the author's anecdote, not a verified history of the term.
- In 059 the sequential advice (correct recitation, Arabic, then translation/tafsir), the original Bengali/English translation context, and the warning against pride or unqualified fatwas are retained. The suggested 4/5-month learning time is identified as the author's estimate.
- In 060 the supplied Arabic for Quran 39:23 says `خُلُودُ` where the [Quranic verse](https://quran.com/39/23) says `جُلُودُ`; the supplied Arabic for 7:205 says `وَجِيفَةً` where the [verse](https://quran.com/7/205) says `وَخِيفَةً`. As required for protected Arabic, the work file reproduces both excerpts exactly; its Urdu meanings follow the Quranic verses. **The Arabic source is therefore not publication-ready** and needs an authorized upstream correction. The 8:2 meaning was checked against [Quran 8:2](https://quran.com/8/2). The hadith citations, gradings and author's interpretation of recitation are retained as attributed source claims, not claimed as independently authenticated.
